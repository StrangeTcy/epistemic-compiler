## Theorist (P01-T), two samples

Two answers to `M04-P01-T v1` arrived together and were stored as a/b in paste order. Both say they saw no other role's output. Sample a has no supplied model label; sample b was prefixed by the user with “opus 5.5”. Both responses self-report Claude, which does not resolve the model identity. Their complete text, including a's preamble, is preserved in `theorist.a.md` and `theorist.b.md`.

**Conformance.** Both contain the ten requested sections. Sample a has two preamble sentences glued to the `ROLE` header, reports “Nothing cut,” and is roughly within the 3,500-word guide. Sample b finishes all ten sections and explicitly lists the formal event-model definitions, discretized global-games oracle, and power calculations as cuts. Arena mode and session time were not supplied for either. The tools and model identities in the response headers are self-reports.

**Checked on 2026-10-02.**

1. **Scripted-Bob oracle (independent exact enumeration).** I enumerated the finite delivery tree using rational probabilities: `m₁` goes to Alice, recipients alternate, the chain stops at the first lost message or after k successful messages, and Bob plays X only if he received `m_k`. For ε=0.1 and even k=8, 10, 12, q(n)=0 at every Alice observation below n=k/2 and q(k/2)=0.9; for odd k, Bob never receives the odd-indexed `m_k`, so q=0 throughout. This confirms sample b's central cutoff diagnosis and contradicts sample a's claim that q is a tail probability increasing across interior n. It also contradicts design §3's formula and its worked interior cutoff. This check treats Alice's observed n as her final receipt count; no unobserved future deliveries occur after the protocol's termination.
2. **Payoff threshold.** On a G history, `EU(X)=qG−(1−q)L`, so the break-even condition is q=L/(G+L). At equality X and Y are both best responses; the current design's `q ≥` convention selects X, while sample a asks for a tie rule (and sample b proposes excluding a registered margin around ties). The choice must be made explicitly at revision/freeze.
3. **PUBLIC rule consistency.** Design §3 states the same scripted rule—Bob plays X iff he received `m_k`—and also sets q=1 for a public announcement of G. A public announcement does not, by itself, mean Bob received `m_k`; without a separate, registered policy or an explicit mapping from PUBLIC to that event, these clauses conflict. Sample b's objection is supported by the written design.
4. **Capped endogenous v2 (rational-arithmetic check).** A second exact type-by-type strict-deletion calculation for k=10, ε=0.1 reproduces sample b's listed survivors under the finite terminal-history/type interpretation: (G,L)=(100,95) leaves both actions at the top types but only Y at interior types; (100,90) and (100,80) leave both actions at every type ≥1; (10,95) leaves only Y. Thus a finite cap can have multiplicity or a knife-edge, unlike the design's blanket v2 sentence. This is conditional on the model's observation/type definitions and strict-deletion rule; the design must specify whether players observe the protocol's termination and exactly what a type contains. The cited unbounded Rubinstein result was not independently checked.
5. **T-EC arithmetic (sample b's proposed repair).** Under its stated nested-knowledge rule, q at the boundary is (1−ε)/(2−ε); the listed boundary expected utilities (+15.8, −15.8, +11.1, −7.7, +11.8) recompute from `qG−(1−q)L`. The thresholds and cutoff shift between r+1 and r+2 follow from whether L is below or above (1−ε)G. The response's shorthand that an opponent is “one lower” applies directly to Alice-oriented type indexing; Bob's receipt-count indexing is same-or-one-higher, so that sentence needs role-specific wording. This checks the arithmetic, not whether T-EC is the best construct.
6. **Graph-pack statements.** Checked against `context/shared.md` and `knowledge/edges.yaml`: the pack includes the BMS node and not a section for `aumann-1976`; edge 008 is from `aumann-brandenburger-1995`; edge 081 does label `rubinstein-1989 — empirically-tests — common-knowledge`. Whether that relation is a sound literature interpretation, or whether the cited papers support the other literature claims in either response, has not been checked against the primary sources.

**Where they agree and differ.** Both say the scripted Bob does not instantiate Rubinstein's strategic, higher-order-belief problem and that the delivery-manipulation/exact-oracle wedge could survive under a narrower or repaired claim. Sample a recommends renaming the primary phenomenon or promoting endogenous v2, but its own interior-tail calculation is inconsistent with the finite stopping rule. Sample b identifies the parity-driven one-cell collapse and PUBLIC-policy contradiction, finds multiplicity in the capped v2 examples, and proposes a different scripted rule (T-EC) to restore an order-by-depth manipulation. T-EC remains a scripted Bob whose action is a nested-knowledge condition; it should not be conflated with endogenous strategic rationalizability.

**What they ask of the seed/design (not applied).** Sample a asks for a claim/title matching the scripted first-order posterior task or for v2 to become primary; exact message-tree enumeration; a frozen tie convention; and better-separated H0/H1/H2/H3 profiles. Sample b asks for a corrected primary mechanism (its T-EC alternative or a carefully parameterized endogenous game), explicit timing/termination and parity conventions, non-tie profiles, a consistent PUBLIC negative control, and controls that separate reading, policy-following, surface cues, and epistemic depth. These are Council recommendations, not decisions. No seed or design revision has been made from them.

**Not checked.** Primary-source claims about Rubinstein, Aumann, DEL/public-announcement logic, Carlsson–van Damme, the cited LLM benchmarks, and novelty; sample a's [V]/[S] literature assertions; sample b's weak-negative search claim. The graph's `empirically-tests` label was verified as present, but its correction remains a literature judgment. No model behavior was tested.

## Experimentalist (P02-E)

One response to `M04-P02-E v1` was pasted and stored as `experimentalist.md`. It self-reports “GPT-5.6 Sol,” browsing available and no code execution, and that it saw no other role output; none of those metadata are independently confirmed. Arena mode, displayed model label and session time were not supplied.

**Conformance.** All ten numbered sections are present; no cuts are declared. It gives a detailed replacement protocol (S04-R1), profile set, renderer, dual-oracle plan, pilot, registered run, analysis and outcome rules. The response is roughly within the prompt's 3,500-word guide. Its two Rubinstein references are tagged `[V]` but have no bibliography or citation trail in the response.

**Checked on 2026-10-02.**

1. **S04-R1 oracle and profiles.** With `q(d)=(1−ε)^(k−d)` and `EU(X)=qG−(1−q)L`, X is strictly preferred when q>L/(G+L), with ties excluded. Exact-fraction checks reproduce all five listed `d_min` values and thresholds. The adjacent-depth margins also pass `.015`; U1 is close, at about `.0150919` at d=4.
2. **Cell and workload totals do not reconcile.** The five k values sum to **51**, not 49, and the table's per-profile cells sum to **153** (8+10+12+10+11 depths, each ×3 covers), not 147. The 72 disagreement cells do check: `3×(2+4+8+3+7)=72`. Given the listed full grid, the core six-arm set is 153×6=918 prompts, plus 72 SCAFFOLD prompts = 990 (plus 15 separately listed PUBLIC-N diagnostics), not 954. The 2,496-episode plan likewise uses 147 rather than 153 base cells; applying its stated repetitions to the listed grid gives 2,592. The hardening denominators should be 153: always-X is 81/153 (52.94%), always-Y is 72/153 (47.06%), and the profile-majority policy is 102/153 (66.67%). No six-cell exclusion is specified; constant-oracle profiles and ties do not account for the discrepancy.
3. **Pilot scaffold count.** In the five profiles' stated anchor-depth lists, the disagreement depths are 2+3+3+3+3=14 before any cover-3 duplicates, not 12. A 300-episode total is arithmetically consistent with deliberately selecting 12 SCAFFOLD cells, but those 12 must be named and the sampling rule specified.
4. **PUBLIC arm semantics.** S04-R1 says PUBLIC replaces Alice's observation with a common board stating G **and that Bob chooses X**. That directly reveals Bob's action, while CHAIN requires inference about completion risk. If the intended contrast is delivery, PUBLIC should convey the corresponding public information/event while retaining the same Bob rule, rather than simply announce his choice. This is a design concern, not an arithmetic error.
5. **Repair's claim ceiling.** The response itself appropriately says S04-R1 tests scripted-partner completion-risk reasoning, not bounded higher-order belief, and asks for endogenous types if the named common-knowledge claim is retained. Its `[V]` statements about the original email game and equilibrium were not independently checked against primary sources.

**What it asks of the seed/design (not applied).** Register S04-R1 instead of the current §3 model, or add a separate endogenous finite-type arm for the named S04 question; exclude ties and fix the nearest-depth margin; specify generator, renderer, paired controls, oracle independence and hardening; correct the 51-depth/153-cell totals and recompute prompt/run budgets; explicitly select the pilot scaffold cells; and repair PUBLIC so it does not disclose Bob's action as an extra cue. It also flags that the Council prompt itself pre-offers the scripted-Bob collapse as a possible verdict. These are recommendations only. No Mission 04 design or seed change has been made.

## Skeptic (P03-S), single response

One response to `M04-P03-S v1` was pasted and stored as `skeptic.md`. The user prefixed it with “fable 5.1”; the response self-reports Claude (Anthropic), browsing used twice, and code execution used once. Arena mode, UI-confirmed model label and session time were not supplied. The response says it saw no other role output.

**Conformance.** All ten numbered sections are present. It explicitly lists cuts (third-oracle pseudocode, more EU margins, Strategy IR discussion); approximately 3,732 response words by whitespace count, slightly above the prompt's ~3,500-word guide. The unusual trailing Markdown hard-break spaces and literal `&lt;` / `&amp;` strings in the paste were retained. Its cited literature claims are tagged but not fully referenced.

**Checked on 2026-10-02.**

1. **Protocol/cutoff diagnosis.** Independent message-tree enumeration already recorded under the Theorist review confirms the core claim: for even k under the ping-pong protocol, q=0 at Alice observations n<k/2 and q=1−ε only at n=k/2; for odd k Bob never receives m_k. The design's one-way tail formula therefore does not match its stated ping-pong process. The Skeptic's n=0 posterior `Pr(G | n=0)=ε/(1+ε)` also checks under prior 1/2 and an initial coordinator message lost with probability ε.
2. **Near-tie example.** At ε=.1, k=10, n′=3, the design sketch gives q=.9^7=.4782969; `L/(G+L)=90/190=.4736842`; `EU(X)=q·190−90≈+0.8764`. The arithmetic is right and the margin is small, so registering an exclusion band is reasonable. It is not an exact tie, however: an exact risk-neutral oracle uniquely selects X; “coin-flip even for a perfect reasoner” is rhetorical unless a rounding/noise model is specified.
3. **Seed/prompt wording flags.** Edge 081 is indeed present in the seed's intuition as `rubinstein-1989 -- empirically-tests --> common-knowledge`; whether that graph relation is a sound literature judgment remains unchecked. The alleged expletive in `cannot_justify` item 3 is **not in the current `mission-04/seed.yaml`** (item 3 is the all/most-frontier-models ceiling), nor does “fuckingly tested” appear in the current Mission 04 seed/design/prompt. The design §4 “Rules out” column states each arm's intended control purpose; it does not report that the controls have already succeeded, so this wording flag overstates the table. The prompt does explicitly invite the scripted-Bob kill attempt, making the leading-wording concern relevant.
4. **Arithmetic-twin proposal.** A matched no-partner survival-probability task is a useful proposed control for separating arithmetic/computation difficulty from partner framing. It is a proposal, not a control in the frozen design. The response's claim that its absence invalidates every public-vs-chain contrast is an interpretation to debate: an arithmetic twin would strengthen construct attribution but does not automatically make delivery manipulation uninterpretable.
5. **One-sided metric and always-Y.** The response correctly notes that an always-Y policy has perfect accuracy on an oracle-Y-only disagreement subset, so a stratum accuracy alone cannot demonstrate discrimination. But it conflates this with `ρ=P(X | oracle Y)`: always-Y gives ρ=0, not a high H1 neglect rate. The current design also includes full-chain accuracy and a cutoff measure. A two-sided hit/false-alarm analysis is still a useful strengthening, but its rationale should distinguish those measures.
6. **Other proposed controls/thresholds.** Padding the PUBLIC arm, a transcript-counting adversary, cutoff mobility across ε/payoff, renderer-derived oracle checks, and explicit comprehension-key validation are plausible design improvements. The response's gate values are not internally harmonized: §6 requires parse ≥.98, COMPREHENSION/PUBLIC ≥.95 and TWIN ≥.90, while §8 aborts at .95/.90/.90/.80 respectively. These may be intended as evidential gates versus stop thresholds, but the distinction and outcomes for the intermediate bands need to be explicit.
7. **Unverified literature.** The experimental-human claims about the Rubinstein game and the cited Cabrales–Nagel–Armenter and Lederman works were not checked against primary sources. The response's broader claim that the literature rejects the all-Y prescription remains unverified.

**Where it agrees and differs.** It agrees with the Theorist and Experimentalist that the current scripted-Bob protocol is not the named higher-order strategic phenomenon and that the stated oracle is inconsistent with ping-pong message delivery. It shares the Experimentalist's concern about computational load, but prefers a no-partner arithmetic twin as a primary comparator; it warns that the Experimentalist's proposed S04-R1 still measures completion-risk reasoning. It adds response-bias, counting-policy, stopping-observability, transcript-position, and prompt-leading critiques. The always-Y critique is partly sound for subset accuracy but overstates the problem for ρ and for the full-chain O5 rule.

**What it asks of the seed/design (not applied).** Rename v1 as delivery-structure/completion-risk sensitivity or add endogenous strategic types for a common-knowledge claim; add an arithmetic twin; use two-sided discrimination and require cutoff movement across ε/payoff; audit transcript leakage and stopping/timing; harmonize gates/abort thresholds; and exclude the near-zero-EU stratum. These are recommendations, not decisions. No Mission 04 design or seed change has been made.

**Not checked.** Rubinstein's theorem and the original game's details; human-subject evidence and the cited 2007/Lederman sources; the novel-claim prior-work search; and the proposition that a risk-neutral oracle is normatively inappropriate for the registered task. No model campaign was run.
