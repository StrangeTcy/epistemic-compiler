**Checked on 2026-10-02.**

1. **Scripted-Bob oracle (independent exact enumeration).** I enumerated the finite delivery tree using rational probabilities: `m₁` goes to Alice, recipients alternate, the chain stops at the first lost message or after k successful messages, and Bob plays X only if he received `m_k`. For ε=0.1 and even k=8, 10, 12, q(n)=0 at every Alice observation below n=k/2 and q(k/2)=0.9; for odd k, Bob never receives the odd-indexed `m_k`, so q=0 throughout. This confirms sample b's central cutoff diagnosis and contradicts sample a's claim that q is a tail probability increasing across interior n. It also contradicts design §3's formula and its worked interior cutoff. This check treats Alice's observed n as her final receipt count; no unobserved future deliveries occur after the protocol's termination.
2. **Payoff threshold.** On a G history, `EU(X)=qG−(1−q)L`, so the break-even condition is q=L/(G+L). At equality X and Y are both best responses; the current design's `q ≥` convention selects X, while sample a asks for a tie rule (and sample b proposes excluding a registered margin around ties). The choice must be made explicitly at revision/freeze.
3. **PUBLIC rule consistency.** Design §3 states the same scripted rule—Bob plays X iff he received `m_k`—and also sets q=1 for a public announcement of G. A public announcement does not, by itself, mean Bob received `m_k`; without a separate, registered policy or an explicit mapping from PUBLIC to that event, these clauses conflict. Sample b's objection is supported by the written design.
4. **Capped endogenous v2 (rational-arithmetic check).** A second exact type-by-type strict-deletion calculation for k=10, ε=0.1 reproduces sample b's listed survivors under the finite terminal-history/type interpretation: (G,L)=(100,95) leaves both actions at the top types but only Y at interior types; (100,90) and (100,80) leave both actions at every type ≥1; (10,95) leaves only Y. Thus a finite cap can have multiplicity or a knife-edge, unlike the design's blanket v2 sentence. This is conditional on the model's observation/type definitions and strict-deletion rule; the design must specify whether players observe the protocol's termination and exactly what a type contains. The cited unbounded Rubinstein result was not independently checked.
5. **T-EC arithmetic (sample b's proposed repair).** Under its stated nested-knowledge rule, q at the boundary is (1−ε)/(2−ε); the listed boundary expected utilities (+15.8, −15.8, +11.1, −7.7, +11.8) recompute from `qG−(1−q)L`. The thresholds and cutoff shift between r+1 and r+2 follow from whether L is below or above (1−ε)G. The response's shorthand that an opponent is “one lower” applies directly to Alice-oriented type indexing; Bob's receipt-count indexing is same-or-one-higher, so that sentence needs role-specific wording. This checks the arithmetic, not whether T-EC is the best construct.
6. **Graph-pack statements.** Checked against `context/shared.md` and `knowledge/edges.yaml`: the pack includes the BMS node and not a section for `aumann-1976`; edge 008 is from `aumann-brandenburger-1995`; edge 081 does label `rubinstein-1989 — empirically-tests — common-knowledge`. ## Compiler follow-up search on prior work (separate from P04-PW)

These checks were run after receiving P04, on 2026-10-02, to cover gaps in its self-reported search budget. They are not part of the verbatim Council response and do not retroactively change it.

1. **Peer-reviewed DEL evaluation beyond static puzzle QA.** Li, Luo & Liao, “Logical reasoning in evolving scenarios: Evaluating LLMs with dynamic epistemic logic puzzles,” *Knowledge-Based Systems* 342 (7 June 2026), article 115885, [DOI 10.1016/j.knosys.2026.115885](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.knosys.2026.115885). The publisher's abstract describes 2,784 samples based on Muddy Children and Cheryl's Birthday, with both answer-prediction and role-playing tasks and difficulty varied via agents/rounds; it explicitly tests recursive knowledge updates after sequential information exchange. This is a strong antecedent for LLM dynamic-epistemic evaluation and directly fills a required search category P04 did not clearly account for. It is still puzzle/role-play evaluation, not an economic choice under a lossy chain or S04's exact payoff cutoff.
2. **Private-information games and multi-turn interaction.** Eisenstein et al., “MT-PingEval: Evaluating Multi-Turn Collaboration with Private Information Games,” [arXiv:2602.24188v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.24188v2) (10 August 2026; first submitted 27 February 2026), evaluates a two-player suite of verifiable tasks using private images/structured data (chess-board progression, COVR, MD3 image selection, tangram and a structured name-game). It generates 1,000 examples per task and tests five model families under two, four, eight or sixteen turns with a fixed per-player token budget. Its formal “level-k interactivity” is the minimum number of dialogue turns needed to communicate enough private information to reach a score threshold; it is not k-th-order epistemic belief or common-knowledge depth. This is direct prior art for LLM multi-turn collaboration with private information and variable interaction budget, but it does not manipulate message loss/public announcements or a payoff cutoff. Treat its result as evidence about turn-use and collaboration, not an exact S04 construct.

4. **Epistemic asymmetry in interactive collaboration (CRAFT and its applied study).** The underlying benchmark is Nath, VanderHoeven & Krishnaswamy, “CRAFT: Grounded Multi-Agent Coordination Under Partial Information,” [arXiv:2603.25268v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25268v2) (28 April 2026).
