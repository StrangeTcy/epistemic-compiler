title: "Same Facts, Different Decisions: Presentation Susceptibility in Mercury-2.5"
...

A model can know the right answer and still act on the wrong one. In a 36-pair controlled probe, Mercury-2.5's stated posterior never changed with presentation order, but its terminal decision changed in 5 pairs and its diagnostic choices in 17.

The probe holds the facts and the oracle fixed; only the order of presentation changes. The model's belief is order-invariant; its behavior is not. This is a presentation-susceptibility finding: the decision layer responds to surface form that the belief layer ignores.

The broader suite confirms the belief layer's competence (23/23 exact posterior match across interventions) while the intervention cases show the decision layer's fragility (visible_proxy pass rate 0.30).

The takeaway for evaluation: score beliefs and decisions separately; a correct posterior is not a correct action.

Caveats apply: small sample, single model, extraction brittleness.
